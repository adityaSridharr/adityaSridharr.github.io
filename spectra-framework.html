When Interpretability Becomes a Liability: Adversarial Attacks on Concept Bottleneck Models

Concept-Level Adversarial Analysis and Robust Training for Interpretable Models

Exposing and Defending the Hidden Attack Surface of Interpretable Models

When Interpretability Becomes a Liability presents the first systematic study of adversarial vulnerabilities in Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs), revealing that interpretability introduces a previously overlooked attack surface at the concept layer itself. While CBMs provide human-understandable intermediate representations, minimal and semantically meaningful perturbations to concept activations can induce catastrophic misclassification failures.

This work introduces a principled framework for concept-level adversarial attacks, deriving closed-form minimal-norm perturbations in concept space and establishing rigorous robustness metrics to quantify semantic vulnerability. To address these weaknesses, the paper proposes SPECTRA (Semantic Perturbation-based Concept Training for Robustness against Adversarial Attacks), a stability-regularized training objective that dramatically increases the cost of successful attacks while preserving classification performance.

Extensive evaluation on the CUB-200-2011 dataset demonstrates robustness improvements of up to 7,600× with less than 0.22% accuracy degradation, establishing concept-level robustness as a critical design consideration for interpretable machine learning systems.

Key Contributions
  • Identified and formalized a novel concept-level adversarial threat model for Concept Bottleneck Models
  • Derived closed-form optimal adversarial perturbations in concept space using optimization theory
  • Introduced the SPECTRA Attackability metric to quantify semantic robustness in interpretable models
  • Proposed stability-regularized training achieving up to 7,600× reduction in attackability
  • Demonstrated robustness gains on CUB-200-2011 (312 concepts, 15 classes) with ≤0.22% accuracy loss

Research Methodology

Problem Identification

Identified a critical gap in adversarial robustness research: the absence of systematic analysis of attacks targeting interpretable concept representations in Concept Bottleneck Models.

Concept-Level Attack Formulation

Formalized concept-space adversarial attacks by analyzing the geometry of concept-to-class mappings and deriving minimal-norm perturbations for both linear and locally linearized classifiers.

Robustness Quantification

Developed comprehensive robustness metrics, including attackability scores and perturbation norms, enabling systematic comparison of CBMs under semantic adversarial threat models.

Defense via Stability Regularization

Introduced SPECTRA, a training-time stability regularization objective that increases the minimal perturbation required for successful attacks while maintaining classification accuracy.

Empirical Validation

Evaluated attacks and defenses on the CUB-200-2011 dataset, revealing a sharp robustness phase transition and identifying optimal operating points for practical deployment.
